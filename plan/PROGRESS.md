## Initiative: Enclosures / Media / Replies (M0–M5)

**Not a numbered ticket, and not specified in any `plan/` document.** It was scoped directly by the founder in a working session on 2026-08-11 after an investigation into the state of media and replies, with explicit product decisions that supersede the investigation's own recommendations. This section exists so a future session can pick the work up without that conversation.

**The founding principle, verbatim from the founder:**

> Every change must strengthen Timely Capsule's core promise: People are paying for greater custody and preservation, not for trust. Trust is never paywalled. Delivery is never paywalled. A sealed capsule keeps every promise that existed when it was sealed.

**Standing process for this initiative:** one milestone at a time; stop after each for review; report what changed, what was reused, backend changes, architectural implications, and verification performed. Do not start the next milestone without approval.

**Status of the branch carrying M0–M3:** `guest-composer-orientation`, five commits, **unpushed and unmerged**. See "Where the work actually is" above.

### M0 — Seal expiry validation · ✅ complete (`ae524bb`, 2026-08-11)

**Purpose.** A capsule whose chosen expiry had lapsed while the composer sat open became permanently unsealable, with no way back.

**Key decisions.** The founder's instruction was explicit: *"Do not patch it with a useEffect that blindly clears state. Treat this as an input-dependent validation state."* Root cause was a `now` frozen at mount, upstream of a *latching* `sealProblem`. The architectural lesson recorded at the time: validation that depends on an input must be **derived** from that input, never **remembered** from a rejection — a remembered rejection needs invalidation logic, and invalidation logic is what creates dead ends. New `composer/v2/schedule-validity.ts` (`describeScheduleProblem`) does the deriving. A second fault was fixed in the same milestone: autosave failures were silent, and the queue could report a retry that could never happen (`use-capsule-draft.ts` gained a `rejected` status distinguishing a 4xx refusal from a transport failure).

**Verification.** Typecheck, lint, production build; live composer walkthrough including a deliberately lapsed moment recovering by itself.

**Open items.** None.

### M1 — Unified enclosure lifecycle + voice recorder · ✅ complete (`9b68a71`, 2026-08-11)

**Purpose.** One lifecycle for everything entering a capsule, and a recorder you decide to keep from.

**Key decisions.** Lifecycle is `Idle → Encrypting → Uploading → Processing → Enclosed → Failed`, surfaced as *readings* — the founder was explicit: *"Do not introduce: progress bars, animated loaders, skeleton shimmer… These are readings, not loading animations."* (`composer/v2/enclosure-lifecycle.ts`.) The recorder (`VoiceRecorder.tsx`) runs `Idle → Recording → Preparing → Ready → Enclosed`, and *"Nothing should become part of the capsule until the writer deliberately accepts it"* — previously pressing stop encrypted and sent the take immediately, so the most personal thing anyone encloses was the only one they could never hear back or redo.

**One deliberate deviation, still open to reversal.** The brief asked for a red **pulse** on the recording indicator. `design/07 §VI.3` bans pulse outright and the ban is enforced by a repo lint rule (`no-restricted-syntax`). The rule was deferred to: a static red dot, with liveness carried by the elapsed timer ticking 5×/sec (the Dual Clock's own idiom, and it survives `prefers-reduced-motion`). Flagged to the founder as requiring their decision if they want it changed.

**Verification.** Typecheck, lint, production build; live enclosing of real files through every stage, including failure and retry.

**Open items.** The pulse deviation above.

### M2 — Unified media viewer · ✅ complete (`787656e`, plus `9e68221`, 2026-08-11)

**Purpose.** One viewer for owner and recipient, web and mobile, across images, video, audio, PDFs and documents.

**What it replaced.** Two viewers, neither of which viewed much: the owner's page called `window.open(blobUrl)` — throwing the artifact outside the letter it belongs to — and the reveal rendered a bare `<img>` for images and a download link for everything else, so **a voice recording could not be played anywhere in the product that received it**. The decrypt call was written twice, meaning the two halves of the product could disagree about how to read the same object.

**Key decisions.** Three rules, documented in `components/enclosure/EnclosureViewer.tsx`: (1) opens **in place, never a modal** — `07 §XVI` bans modals on the wait/reveal path, and a lightbox over a letter turns the letter into a gallery; (2) the chrome is a **museum label, not a file browser** — kind, size, and the sender's note as the plate, with no filename heading, toolbar or zoom furniture; (3) **nothing opens itself** — decryption is work and the bytes are the reader's to ask for, which also stops a page of enclosures decrypting a hundred megabytes nobody asked to see. Opening mirrors M1's enclosing: a named, held step, never a spinner. `lib/enclosure-media.ts` collapses the duplicated decrypt path and adds `enclosureNoun`/`enclosureGlyph` so no glyph appears without a label (`07 §XII`).

**A latent reveal-path bug found and fixed while verifying (`9e68221`).** `useTransit`'s `skip()` set a ref and left the jump to the next `requestAnimationFrame` — inert whenever rAF is suspended (backgrounded tab, bfcache restore, low-power throttling), which is exactly when a stalled ceremony makes someone reach for the skip. A recipient tapping "show the letter now" on a stalled transit got nothing, so `07 §V.7`'s guarantee that the document is never gated by the animation did not hold. `skip` now sets the timeline scalar directly. Predates M2; the same file's comments already flagged rAF-in-background as a hazard for *replay* but it had never been fixed for skip.

**Verification.** Owner path and recipient reveal path both exercised end to end with genuinely decodable media generated in-browser: image decoded 640×360, video 320×180 at `readyState 4`, audio played with `currentTime` advancing and a non-empty `played` range, a real PDF in its object frame, a `.docx` falling through to "take a copy". 390px, zero horizontal overflow. Typecheck, lint, production build, full API suite.

**Open items.** None.

### M3 — Encrypted replies / correspondence · ✅ complete (`4cc648c`, 2026-08-12)

**Purpose.** The other half of the correspondence, sealed like the first.

**What the audit found.** Four faults, of which the first is a hard-rule violation: (1) **replies were stored in plaintext** — `reactions.note` was a `text` column, so every reply anyone had written sat in the database readable by us, in a product whose first hard rule is that capsule contents never reach servers in plaintext; (2) the text leaked further, going verbatim into the notification title, the notification body, and the email — and since sending a template also persists its props to the outbound ledger, into the database a second time; (3) **nothing could read it** — no code anywhere selected from `reactions`, so the feature was write-only and the sender could never see a reply; (4) **guest replies were dropped** — `if (capsule.ownerId)` guarded the whole path, so a reply to a guest-sealed capsule was silently swallowed.

**Key decisions.** New `capsule_replies` table (migration `0012_simple_cargill.sql`, additive; the old `reactions` table is retained with its rows and marked deprecated, nothing writes to it). Bodies are encrypted with the **capsule key**, which required no new key exchange because both parties already hold it — the recipient in the `#k=` fragment of their link, the sender by unwrapping `ownerKeyWrap` with their master key. `recipientId` is the thread: a capsule addressed to four people is four correspondences, not a group chat. Both sides can write, so it is a conversation rather than a comment box, and both sides render the same `components/reply/Correspondence.tsx` — **deliberately not a messaging UI**: no bubbles, no avatars, no alignment-by-author, no unread dots. Replies live inside the record, after the enclosures, because an answer severed from the thing it answers is close to meaningless ten years on. The five canned phrases survive but now **fill the composer instead of sending on click** — previously the commonest way to use the feature was to fire an unchangeable four-word message with one touch, discarding anything typed afterwards. Guests read their replies through their session token (`guestRepliesRouter`), since a guest-sealed capsule has no `ownerId` and the authenticated route can never serve it; **read-only**, because a guest has no master key to hold a conversation from.

**Verification.** Full flow on real capsules: recipient replies → ciphertext-only row in the database → notification reading "replied" with a **null** body → email payload carrying only a name and a URL → sender unlocks → reads decrypted text → answers → both sides see the same two-turn thread correctly attributed → survives reload. Guest path proven on a genuinely guest-sealed capsule with a null `owner_id`. 390px, zero overflow. Seven new API tests pin the guarantees, including asserting no notification or ledger row can contain the ciphertext. 243/243 API tests, typecheck, lint, production build.

**Open items.** None.

### M4 — Storage lifecycle · ✅ complete (2026-08-12)

**Purpose.** Make archival mean *"reclaim the bytes, keep the record"*, and make a capsule's retention behaviour a property of the capsule rather than of the owner's current subscription.

**The founder's decisions, taken before implementation and locked:** terminology (**Archive** stays the capsule shelf; **Release** is media leaving storage); Free = 100 MB + **1-year media retention after unlock**; **two** warnings (30d and 7d) as separate scheduled actions; quota **Option A** (live quota counts sealed media and gates new writes only); **capsule-level manual release only**; encrypted replies **included** in export; legacy (null-snapshot) capsules = **forever**, never backfilled.

**`mediaBytesAtSeal` was dropped on the founder's challenge, correctly.** The snapshot records the *promise*, not an observation of contents, and the figure is derivable forever from the media rows — whose `size_bytes` survive release precisely so the account can still say what it once held.

#### What was built

- **`capsules.sealed_entitlement_snapshot`** (jsonb) + **`media_retention_until`** (an *index* of it, written in the same statement). **`media_items.released_at`/`release_reason`**, `media_status += released`, `action_kind += media_release{,_warning}`. Migration `0013_quiet_bug.sql`, additive only.
- **`modules/capsules/sealed-entitlement.ts`** — the reader boundary. `planAtSeal` is stored for humans but is **deliberately unreachable through any exported accessor**, so `if (plan === 'premium')` is awkward to write rather than merely discouraged. Unknown future `schemaVersion` reads as `forever`: the only safe direction to be wrong in.
- **`mediaRetention.afterUnlockMs` is stored alongside the resolved date.** Found while implementing: `extendCapsule` moves `unlockAt` by up to 30 days, and the promise is *"a year after it opens"* — so extend re-anchors the date from the stored term. Without the term, a short enough retention could violate `retentionUntil >= unlockAt`.
- **`assertCanReleaseMedia`** — a *sibling* of `assertCanPatch`, never a relaxation of it. Sealed/unlocked/expired only; draft rejected (delete is the right verb there), withdrawn/trashed rejected (already bound for `purgeCapsuleNow`).
- **`releaseCapsuleMedia`** — **R2 delete first, DB update second.** That ordering is load-bearing: a `released` row is then *proof* the delete returned successfully, and a retry converges because S3 DELETE is idempotent. The reverse ordering would strand paid-for bytes with nothing in the system aware of it. Per-item failures are collected, not thrown, so one bad object cannot strand the other nine.
- **Two scheduled kinds**, not one — both carry `subjectId=capsuleId`, so `reminder`'s trick of probing two disjoint UUID spaces was unavailable. Handlers re-derive their own authority from the capsule rather than trusting that a row exists.
- **`entitlements.storage_quota_bytes` wired up.** It was a real `notNull` column that **nothing had ever read**. It is now the per-user override, which is the entire seam storage packs need — a pack becomes a number, never a branch on a plan name.
- **Export made honest.** Three silent-skip paths removed; every media row now emits an entry with `exportStatus` (`included`/`released`/`incomplete`/`unavailable`). Encrypted replies exported as `replies/*.enc`, the same convention as `letters/*.enc`. **A README was added — the export had never had one.**
- **Reveal no longer filters to `ready`.** It did, which meant a released enclosure would have vanished from the recipient's letter with nothing in its place. `RevealMedia.viewUrl` is now nullable with `released`/`releasedAt` alongside. The recipient's "keep a copy" zip names released files in `released.txt` rather than quietly shipping fewer files than the letter described.
- **Owner surface:** `getMediaViewUrl` returns a new `media_released` (410) instead of the flat lie *"still uploading or processing"*; `EnclosureViewer` gained a released state that keeps the museum label and drops the dead Open button.
- **Storage settings rebuilt as a reading** — progress bar removed, released volume and next release date stated, and the snapshot-vs-live distinction spelled out in text rather than implied.

#### Delivery safety, enforced mechanically

Grep-verified that no delivery handler imports any entitlement symbol, then made it permanent: an ESLint `no-restricted-imports` rule on `capsule-unlock.ts`/`recipient-unlock.ts`. **Proven by deliberately adding the forbidden import and confirming lint fails**, then reverting. Plus the adversarial test: seal → drop the owner to zero quota → run `capsule_unlock` → capsule still unlocks and the recipient notification is still spawned.

#### Verification — real vs. faked, stated plainly

**Real, against live infrastructure:** a throwaway script (not committed, T-10/T-14 precedent) drove the genuine `R2StorageAdapter` against the real `tc-media` bucket — put a real object, sealed with a real snapshot, ran release, and **confirmed `headObject` returns null**, i.e. the encrypted object is actually gone from Cloudflare. Same run confirmed the media row survives with name/size intact and `storage_key` nulled, the letter and replies survive, storage is genuinely reclaimed, and a duplicate release is a no-op that does not double-decrement.

**Real, in a browser:** storage settings and the released enclosure on the owner's capsule page, at 390px, zero horizontal overflow.

**Faked, and deliberately so:** the 259-test suite runs against `FakeStorageAdapter` (NODE_ENV=test always does, by design since T-10). Partial-failure/retry is exercised by injecting a throwing `deleteObject` — real logic, simulated outage. The recipient's *browser* rendering of a released enclosure was not separately driven; the API response was asserted directly and the component is the same one verified on the owner surface.

**Gate:** 259/259 API tests (36 files, +16 new), typecheck clean on `@tc/api`/`@tc/web`, lint clean across all 8 packages, production `next build` succeeds.

#### Post-implementation review (same session, before commit)

A focused review of the M4 diff against `origin/main`: scope confirmed clean (no M5 guest work, no billing, no unrelated Dashboard/composer changes), product contract re-verified line by line against the code, and the delivery boundary re-proven by deliberately adding the forbidden import and watching lint fail.

- **Chunked stream framing (`encryptStream`/`decryptStream`) authenticates both chunk position and stream completeness.** Initially shipped with only index-bound AAD (stops reordering/swapping) and no anti-truncation check — flagged as a known gap in README.md rather than silently left out, since the plan's T-07b DoD list didn't explicitly ask for it. Closed immediately after: each chunk's AAD now also carries a 1-byte `isFinal` flag, and both `encryptStream` and `decryptStream` use a one-chunk lookahead (peek at `iterator.next()` before yielding/decrypting the current chunk) to know whether the current chunk is genuinely the last one. A transport that drops the true final chunk(s) makes the last-arriving chunk get decrypted as if `isFinal=true`, but it was encrypted with `isFinal=false` — GCM authentication fails, so truncation surfaces as a thrown error instead of silently short plaintext. Two new tests (`aes.test.ts`): one proves a truncated stream throws, one proves a genuinely single-chunk stream still decrypts fine (guards against the fix being over-eager and flagging legitimate short streams as truncated).

- **The 1GB streaming memory-budget test initially timed out at 120s** — not because the crypto was slow (WebCrypto's AES-GCM handled the full 2GB of encrypt+decrypt work in ~2.3s once isolated), but because `vitest`'s `toEqual` deep-equality matcher is drastically slower than a manual loop when repeatedly comparing multi-megabyte `Uint8Array`s (256 chunks × 4 MiB).
