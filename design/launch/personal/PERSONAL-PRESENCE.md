## 0 · Marketing rules this pack is checked against

Derived from `design/07-EXPERIENCE-BIBLE.md` Part VIII and demonstrated in
the current `design/launch/COPY.md` (the 2026-08-25 rewrite — shorter,
wittier, more specific than the pack before it; **do not revert toward the
older, more formal register**).

1. No exclamation marks. No emoji. Nowhere in this pack.
2. **The institution never says "we/our/us."** This governs TimelyCapsule's
   own voice (the company page, the company posts) — third person only
   ("TimelyCapsule keeps...", "the record is destroyed..."). It does **not**
   apply to Assad's personal voice — a real person saying "I built this" is
   correct and expected, not a violation. Conflating the two would make the
   personal posts read as oddly impersonal, which defeats their purpose.
3. Dates literal and absolute; relative time may accompany, never replace.
4. Never "just." Never sell — state.
5. No manufactured urgency, no fake customers/traction/funding/press/awards,
   no testimonials, no engagement-bait questions, no hashtag spam (zero on
   X, ≤2 on Instagram, none needed on LinkedIn).
6. Vocabulary: seal/sealed, record, deliver/delivered, travelled/remaining,
   kept, recipient, the wait — never save/lock, post/content, send/push,
   progress/ETA, stored, user, the countdown.
7. Banned phrases, explicit: "I'm excited to announce," "game changer,"
   "revolutionary," "disrupting," "the future of," "journey," "thrilled,"
   "humbled," "Founder | CEO | Entrepreneur | Startup Guy | Building in
   Public" as a headline formula.
8. Platform limits, verified per-string below, not assumed: X bio ≤160, X
   post ≤280, Instagram bio ≤150, GitHub bio ≤160, LinkedIn headline ≤220,
   LinkedIn About ≤2600 (only ~300 visible before "see more" — front-load
   it), LinkedIn post ≤3000 (ideal 900–1300).
9. No invented product capabilities, no claims the current app can't back up
   — every feature mentioned below was verified hands-on this session or the
   one before it (`design/launch/REFRESH-2026-08-25-NOTES.md` §4).

## 1 · Positioning systems — evaluated

Eight systems considered. Format: how each reads per platform, then
credibility / genericness / TC-connection / brand-fit.

### A. "Founder, TimelyCapsule"
LinkedIn: expected, safe, indistinguishable from a thousand other headlines.
X: says nothing distinctive. Instagram: over-formal for the platform.
GitHub: flat as a bio line, says nothing about the engineer.
**Credible:** yes. **Generic:** very — this is the exact energy being
avoided. **TC-connection:** total. **Brand fit:** poor — TimelyCapsule's own
voice is dry and specific; "Founder, X" is a title card, not a voice.

### B. "Building TimelyCapsule"
LinkedIn: active, humble, but alone doesn't establish *what Assad does* —
needs pairing with a role. X: works well, present-tense. Instagram: fine,
compact. GitHub: weak alone, better paired with "Software engineer."
**Credible:** medium. **Generic:** moderate — "Building @X" is itself a
common bio pattern now, though far better than "Founder." **TC-connection:**
strong. **Brand fit:** decent.

### C. "Software engineer building TimelyCapsule"
LinkedIn: natural headline, credible — leads with craft, not authority.
X: slightly long alone but fine as one clause. Instagram: works.
GitHub: excellent, close to the existing real bio ("Software Engineer.").
**Credible:** highest of the eight — leads with skill, not title.
**Generic:** the *structure* ("[role] building [product]") is a known
pattern, moderately common, but not a cliché the way "Founder" is.
**TC-connection:** clear and specific. **Brand fit:** good — matches the
relationship you described wanting exactly: engineer → building →
TimelyCapsule, not personal-brand → TimelyCapsule.

### D. "Founder / Builder, TimelyCapsule"
The slash construction reads as hedging between two framings rather than
choosing one. It's also, on its own, an extremely common LinkedIn headline
shape at this point — closer to cliché than either half alone. **Not
recommended.**

### E. "TimelyCapsule — letters that arrive later" (no role stated)
LinkedIn: doesn't say what Assad *does* — reads as a company tagline
borrowed as a personal headline, more ad than identity. Fails on GitHub
entirely (bios there are about the person's skills). **Credible:** doesn't
address it. **Generic:** no — distinctive, but for the product's voice, not
Assad's. **TC-connection:** total, but *only* TC — this is the "personal
brand *is* the product" failure mode you explicitly want to avoid. **Not
recommended as a headline** — excellent as a line *inside* an About section
or post, just not as the identity itself.

### F. "Software engineer. Currently: TimelyCapsule."
LinkedIn: terse, confident, borrows the brand's own "shipping manifest"
register (`07 §VIII`: "plain, exact, unhurried") without being a literal TC
tagline — distinctive *because* it sounds like a fact being stated, not a
personality being performed. X: excellent, short, reads as a real person.
Instagram: a little dry for the platform's usual tone, but that dryness
reads as confident understatement, which is the actual goal. GitHub:
natural evolution of the existing real bio.
**Credible:** yes, leads with role. **Generic:** no — the "Currently:"
construction as a standalone fragment is less common than "Building X" and
reads more considered. **TC-connection:** clear without shouting.
**Brand fit:** best of the eight — it's the only option that sounds like it
was written by the same person who wrote `COPY.md`, without quoting it.

### G. "Engineer. Time, mostly."
Too vague — fails the "obvious enough to find" requirement. A stranger
reading this has no route to TimelyCapsule without already knowing it
exists. Clever without being useful. **Not recommended as a headline** —
could work as a single line inside a longer About section, once TimelyCapsule
has already been named once.

### H. "Building things that wait. Currently: TimelyCapsule."
Interesting and thematically tight (TimelyCapsule *is* a thing that waits),
but "things that wait" takes a beat to parse cold, which costs it on a
platform read at a glance. Strong as an About-section opening line, once
paired with the plain version underneath it. Not strong enough alone as the
public-facing identity.

### Recommendation

**Primary: F — "Software engineer. Currently: TimelyCapsule."**
Most credible, least generic, works unmodified in spirit across all four
platforms (adjusted only in length/register per platform below), and is the
only option that sounds native to the brand without copying it.

**Backup: C — "Software engineer building TimelyCapsule."**
Slightly more conventional and slightly clearer at a glance — the safer
choice if F ever reads as too clipped in a specific context (LinkedIn's
"About" opening line, for instance, where C's fuller sentence flows better
than F's two fragments).

## 4 · LinkedIn company posts (9)

Six of nine pair with an existing real-UI asset (§5); three are text-only,
which LinkedIn handles natively and which keeps the set from feeling like

### Post 1 · Why this exists
**Image:** `x/post-mark.png` (reuse, 1600×900)
```
Why this exists

Most software wants you back tomorrow. A notification, a streak, a reason to open the app again before you forget it exists.

TimelyCapsule is built to be forgotten. You write something, choose a date — a month out, or 2041 — and from that point the product's only job is to do absolutely nothing until that date arrives. No nudge. No "come back and see." The waiting itself doesn't need you.

That's a strange thing to optimize a product for. It also seems like the only honest way to build something that promises to hold a letter for ten years.
```

### Post 2 · The part I actually care about
**Image:** `screenshots/06-survival.png` (reuse, 1600×900)
```
The part I actually care about: I can't read your letters

Every capsule is encrypted on the device that writes it, before any of it reaches a server. What arrives at TimelyCapsule's database is ciphertext — no plaintext copy exists anywhere else, not in a log, not in a backup, not in a support tool.

Practically: there's no "reset your password and we'll help recover your data" flow the way most products have one. Lose your key, and the content is unrecoverable — including by me. Not a caveat. The actual design.
```

### Post 3 · What "nothing is happening" looks like
**Image:** `instagram/12-waiting.png` (reuse, 1080×1350)
```
What "nothing is happening" actually looks like

This is a real screenshot of a letter, three years into a ten-year wait. No progress bar animating toward a goal, no "you're 30% of the way there" — just two numbers, travelled and remaining, and a date.

The test I hold this screen to: if a capsule sitting untouched for three years reads as empty instead of kept, the design has failed.

Most software that shows you "nothing is happening" is broken. This one is working exactly as intended.
```

### Post 4 · Refusing the growth-team playbook
**Image:** `instagram/07-refusals.png` (reuse, 1080×1350)
```
Refusing the growth-team playbook, on purpose

There's a well-understood toolkit for making people open an app more often: streaks, badges, "on this day" memories, progress bars, push notifications timed for re-engagement.

None of it is in TimelyCapsule, and none of it is going to be. A sealed letter isn't content to be surfaced back to you — it's something you asked to be left alone until a specific date. Nudging you before then would be a small, quiet betrayal of the one promise the product makes.
```

### Post 5 · What belongs inside a letter
**Image:** `instagram/11-sealing.png` (reuse, 1080×1350)
```
What belongs inside a letter, besides words

A letter in TimelyCapsule can carry more than text: a photo, the coordinates of where it was written, or a voice note — recorded, played back, and only enclosed once you've actually decided to keep it. Nothing attaches automatically the moment you stop recording; you hear it back first.

The voice note is the one I think about most. A photo preserves what something looked like. A voice note preserves how someone actually sounded saying a thing — cadence, the pause, the parts a transcript would flatten out. A different, harder kind of keeping.
```

### Post 6 · The letter doesn't have to be the last word
**Image:** `instagram/14-replies.png` (reuse, 1080×1350)
```
The letter doesn't have to be the last word

I didn't plan to build a reply feature. But every early conversation about this product hit the same question: what if they want to write back?

A capsule's recipient can now reply — encrypted the identical way the original letter was, so the answer is exactly as unreadable to me as the letter that prompted it. Not a message thread. No read receipt, no typing indicator, no notification with a content preview. A second letter, answering the first, on its own schedule.
```

### Post 7 · A feature whose entire job is to destroy something
**Image:** `instagram/15-one-reading-only.png` (reuse, 1080×1350)
```
A feature whose entire job is to destroy something

Most products treat "delete" as an edge case. TimelyCapsule has a mode called "one reading only" where destruction is the entire point: the moment it's opened and read, the record is gone. Not archived. Not soft-deleted with a recovery window. Gone — no copy anywhere, including in any backup.

Longer than I'd like to admit went into making sure that fires only after a confirmed read — not a page load, a prefetch, or a link-preview crawler. That needed a dedicated test suite before I trusted it enough to ship.
```

### Post 8 · A bug that only existed because a fix was too good
**Image:** none — text only, on purpose
```
A bug that only existed because the fix for a different bug was too good

Shipped a fix recently so a logged-in user's dark-mode preference would stick properly on the marketing pages. Verified it worked. Moved on.

Two days later: every hard refresh of a logged-in page was bouncing real, authenticated users straight back to the login screen — a session with valid tokens sitting right there, ignored.

The cause: a loading-state variable that used to safely default to "false" (not loading) now needed a third state — "haven't checked yet" — and defaulting it to false meant the route guard concluded "not loading, no user, redirect" before the code that reads the tokens ever ran. False and unknown had been silently treated as the same value.

Fixed by giving it three real states instead of two. A very small diff for how long it took to notice.
```

### 7.1 · LinkedIn (personal)

**P1 — What most of the actual work is**
```
Somewhere around the third rewrite of the encryption flow, I realized the interesting problem was never "how do I build a time capsule app." It was "how do I build something where the correct answer to almost every feature request is no."

No read receipts. No "seen by." No preview of what's inside before it opens. No way for me — the person who runs the servers — to see what anyone wrote. Every one of those is a feature I chose not to build, on purpose, more than once.

That's most of what I actually do most days now — not adding things, holding a line on what doesn't get added. Stranger kind of engineering work than I expected. I like it more than I thought I would.
```

**P2 — Shipping replies**
```
Shipped replies to TimelyCapsule this week — a capsule's recipient can now write back, sealed the same way the original letter was.

The interesting part wasn't the crypto (mostly reused work I'd already done). It was resisting the urge to make it feel like every other reply feature I've built before: no read receipt, no typing indicator, no notification with a content preview. It just says someone replied. You still have to go open it to find out what they said — same as the letter that started it.

Staying consistent with a product's own principles is more work than building the feature.
```

**P3 — Testing a delete button**
```
Wrote a whole test suite this week for a feature whose only job is to delete something.

TimelyCapsule has a "one reading only" mode — read it once, and the record is destroyed, no copy anywhere. The hard part wasn't the deletion logic. It was making sure it could never fire by accident: not on a page load, not on a link-preview bot crawling the URL before a real person opens it, not on a browser prefetch.

Confirmed-read only, verified against an actual test suite — not eyeballed once and shipped. Destructive features earn a different level of paranoia than the rest of the codebase.
```

**P4 — On making a decade feel true**
```
A decade is 3,653 days. Written down like that it barely registers — small enough that a calendar app could show you a countdown without embarrassing itself.

Most of what I've actually worked on this year has been trying to make that distance feel true inside an interface, instead of just numerically correct. Two numbers — travelled, remaining — turned out to do more of that work than any animation would have.
```

**P5 — Building alone**
```
Nobody warns you that building alone means you're also QA, support, and the person who has to explain to yourself why a decision from four months ago made sense at the time.

Started keeping a written log of decisions as I make them — not for anyone else, just so future-me has something better to work from than "I'm sure I had a reason." Small habit. Has already saved me from re-litigating two decisions I'd already made correctly once.
```
