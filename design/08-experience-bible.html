Meridian · Creative Foundation · Phase 07

The Experience Bible

Five documents answered what it should look like. This one answers what it should feel like — and finds that the answer cannot come from anything we can draw.

Part I · Manifesto

We cannot be recognised the way our references are recognised

The brief is to be identifiable in seconds, like Apple, Linear, Stripe, Arc, Nothing. So look at how each is actually recognised.

BrandRecognised byMechanismOpen to us
NothingDot-matrix type, transparencyOne gimmick, no exceptionsno — decoration
ArcColour, spatial playPersonality through motionno — spectacle
AppleScroll-driven product cinemaSpectacle, expensively madeno — spectacle
StripeGradient, graceful densityCraft at high volumepartly
LinearZero latency, keyboard-firstSpeedno — taken

We forbade ourselves four of those five in 01–05. And we cannot own speed: Linear owns it, and our premise is that some things are worth waiting for. Recognisability cannot come from addition, and it cannot come from being fast.

The thesis

We will be recognised by our timing. Not our colour, not our type, not our motion — our rhythm.

Every product on earth has a uniform tempo: press a thing, it answers in 100ms, always. Ours is bimodal, and the split carries meaning.

Everything reversible is instant — settings, navigation, typing, search. No slower than Linear; reverence in a settings pane is not reverence, it is a slow app. Everything irreversible is held: it stops, completely, before it happens.

Nobody owns this. It is perceptible within seconds. It cannot be screenshotted, so it cannot be casually copied. And unlike a gimmick it gets more meaningful with use, because the rhythm is quietly teaching you which of your actions can be taken back.

The rule that decides every timing in the product

Duration is a function of consequence, never of aesthetics.

If you can undo it, it is instant. If you cannot, it is held. Any timing that cannot be justified by that sentence is decoration, and gets cut.

The signature, made tangible

The Held Beat

This document argues that timing is the identity, so it should let you feel it once — and otherwise hold still. Press both. The difference between them is the brand.

Draft
idle

Renaming is reversible, so it resolves in 0ms — no transition at all. Sealing cannot be undone, so the marker travels at constant velocity, stops dead for 400ms, and only then flips state instantly. A seal is either intact or it isn't; that is never a tween.

The stop is the whole thing. Every animation library — and every engineer — will want to peak there. Stopping instead is what turns a transition into a threshold.

Part II · Creative Principles

Ten decision procedures

  1. 01Duration is a function of consequence. The whole system in one line.
  2. 02The institution never changes; the instruments always do. Return after six months: the building is identical, the readings are different.
  3. 03Behave, don't depict. No hourglass, no vault, no clock face. The moment we illustrate our metaphor we admit the interface isn't carrying it.
  4. 04The record is an object, never a background. Bounded on all four sides, at every breakpoint, forever. (Gate 1)
  5. 05Silence is a design material — and the one we spend most. The likeliest thing to be "fixed" by well-meaning contributors.
  6. 06Certainty before feeling. Anticipation is pleasant only when the outcome is not in doubt. No soft language about delivery, ever.
  7. 07The moment belongs to the recipient. No branding in the reveal. We are not in the room.
  8. 08Never simulate what we can state. No animated "encrypting…" theatre. Security theatre is the fastest way to lose the trust the product runs on.
  9. 09Design for the worst day. The composer may be dying; the recipient may be grieving. Grief impairs attention — one decision per screen at every loaded moment.
  10. 10Reliability is the marketing. Nobody tells a friend about our easing curves. They tell a friend that it arrived.

Almost all of our craft has gone into phase 6, which lasts six seconds. Phase 2 is where people actually struggle, and where they abandon.

A blank field addressed to your daughter in fourteen years is one of the harder things software has ever asked of anybody. People open the composer and freeze — not because the UI is unclear, but because the task is emotionally enormous.

We have designed a magnificent reveal for letters that never got written. This is the largest gap in the work so far, and it now outranks the logo and the design system.

Part V · Interaction Constitution

Twelve articles

  1. 01Reversible is instant; irreversible is held. No exceptions.
  2. 02One decision per screen at any emotionally loaded moment.
  3. 03Never surface a memory without consent. No "on this day", no unbidden excerpt, no preview of sealed content anywhere.
  4. 04State is always stated in text — never in colour, position or motion alone.
  5. 05Ceremony is skippable at any point, always, without penalty.
  6. 06Ceremony never repeats. Returning to an opened capsule shows the record at rest.
  7. 07But re-reading is trivial and unlimited. Article 6 governs the animation, never the document. Someone re-reading a letter from a dead parent must never be made to wait.
  8. 08No modal ever interrupts the wait or the reveal. Not consent, not upsell, not a review prompt.
  9. 09The recipient path assumes nothing — no account, no app, no modern browser, no prior exposure to our conventions.
  10. 10Errors state what happened and what happens next, in the record register. A delivery problem is an institutional failure and is written like one.
  11. 11Nothing consequential is ever optimistic. We do not show success before we have it.
  12. 12No dark patterns at any scale. We forfeited the urgency toolkit permanently.

The held beat has three mandatory parts: an approach at constant velocity (never easing out — easing out anticipates arrival, and the point is that arrival is not yet), the stop at total stillness, and the consequence as an instant state change.

  1. 01Constant velocity by default. Springs, bounces and overshoot permanently banned — materials in an instrument do not bounce.
  2. 02Nothing may read as loading. No spinners, progress bars, shimmer or skeleton pulse.
  3. 03Silence is timing, not motion — so it survives prefers-reduced-motion. The reduced-motion version is arguably the purer one.
  4. 04The mark never animates. Institution is static.
  5. 05Registers never overlap in time. Instrument clears → empty frame → record arrives. (Gate 1)
  6. 06CSS unless proven otherwise. Gate 1 built the full reveal from one scalar. WebGL remains permanently out.
  7. 07Cap every glow at 6% opacity. Lighting effects grow every time somebody touches them.
Part VII · Sound

There is no sound. That is the sound design.

A reveal happens on an unknown device, in an unknown room, at an unknown moment. It may be a commute, an open-plan office, a hospital ward, a funeral. We have no idea, we cannot ask, and the recipient has no relationship with us that would license us to make noise in their space.

This reverses 06, which proposed one mechanical release at the seal, default off. The single narrow exception, if ever requested: haptics on the seal, on the creator's own device, in an interaction they initiated. Never on the reveal. Never audio.

Part VIII · Copy

The register of an institution that expects to outlive you

Closer to a land registry than a greetings card. State, never sell. No exclamation marks, no emoji, ever — in product, notification, email or marketing. One of the cheapest recognisability wins available.

Never use the word "just" — it minimises, and nothing here is small. Dates are always literal and absolute; relative time may accompany a date, never replace one.

Part IX · Composition

The underweighted phase

Forbidden: templates, prompts-as-content, AI-written suggestions, mood pickers — anything producing a letter the person did not write. The value of the artifact is that it is theirs. We would be automating away the only thing that matters.

Available instead:

  • Address the reader before the writing. The recipient's future age is the single most affecting piece of arithmetic we can perform.
  • Questions, not templates. "What do you want them to know that you might not say out loud?" — a question produces the person's own words; a template produces ours. One at a time, dismissible, never inserted into the document.
  • Resumable without shame. A draft sitting for three weeks is normal. No streaks, no nudges, no "you left something unfinished".
  • Sealing separable from writing. The decision to commit is its own emotional act, never a side-effect of finishing a sentence.
Part X · Landing · T-21

Answer the only question that matters

Not "what does it do". "Will you still exist?" Every other objection is downstream. A person deciding whether to write to their daughter in fourteen years is evaluating our institutional durability, not our feature set.

  • A thesis, not a hero. The opening should be a dual clock — a real duration, moving, unexplained. Not a headline about memories.
  • Compress the wait. The one place we may accelerate time, and the only permitted spectacle in the entire product.
  • Answer survival with evidence. Escrow, export guarantees, what happens if the company dies. Boring, specific, and the most persuasive section on the page — most competitors will not dare.
  • Show the record, not the app. No device mockups, no floating UI.
  • No testimonials at launch. We have not kept anything yet.

The unusual rule: the marketing site must be slower than the app — in tempo, not performance. It is the only surface allowed to hold a beat for effect rather than for consequence, because its job is to transmit a feeling rather than execute a task.

Part XI · Onboarding · Notification · Empty · Loading

Four sends in a decade

Onboarding: there isn't one. No tour, no checklist, no sample capsule. The first act is composition. What we owe instead is the master-key and recovery-kit moment handled with total clarity — the one genuinely unfamiliar concept, and the one place where confusion is unrecoverable.

NotificationWhenContains
1SealedimmediateA receipt. The only immediate one.
2Proof of liferareStill here, still counting, nothing for you to do. No CTA, no metric, no streak.
3ApproachonceFactual, to the recipient, close to transit.
4DeliveredtransitThe arrival.

Everything else is forbidden — anniversaries, re-engagement, feature announcements, and anything surfacing a memory the person did not ask to see.

Loading: we mostly should not have any. Where unavoidable, layout-stable placeholders with no shimmer and no pulse. If a surface genuinely needs a loading indicator, that is a performance bug filed against engineering, not a design problem to be decorated.

Part XII · Imagery

We photograph nothing we did not make

Photography: objects only, never people. Instruments, paper, seals, mechanisms — shot flat and forensically, evenly lit, like a museum accession photograph or an auction catalogue plate. Never a model looking wistfully at a phone.

Illustration: none, permanently. Icons: engraved marks, single weight, unrounded, no filled variants, never unlabelled at a consequential moment. The imagery of the product is its own artifacts — the record, the stamp, the seal, the clock.

Part XIII · Accessibility & Performance

Accessibility is the trust argument

A product whose entire premise is this will reach the person you intend cannot ship a reveal that fails for someone using a screen reader. That is not an edge case; it is the product failing at the only thing it does.

WCAG AA minimum, computed and never estimated — the corrected ramp in 05 is the precedent: eyeballing shipped a 2.72 contrast into the most-viewed component. All held beats preserved under reduced motion. That the recipient path is our most accessible surface — paper, serif, high contrast, no motion — is by design, not coincidence.

Performance is a trust signal. A slow product does not feel like it will last a decade. The reveal must work on a three-year-old Android over a poor connection, which is why WebGL is permanently out.

Part XV · Challenges

Seven, ordered by what they cost if ignored

1 · The name works against the brand unresolved

We have built an identity that is grave, institutional and horological, for a product called TimelyCapsule. Timely means punctual or opportune — a business-software word, and mildly ironic for a product about long waits. The compound is a SaaS name, and it will fight every piece of design we make.

You cannot be recognised in three seconds if the first word people read contradicts everything the next three seconds say. This is a founder decision, not a design one — but it is the largest unaddressed threat to the goal in this brief, and it gets harder to change every week.

2 · "Meridian" and "TimelyCapsule" are not reconciled unresolved

02 calls Meridian the institution; 05 uses it as the design-system name. Meridian is currently both the brand and a subsystem, and we will propagate that contradiction into token names, component names and marketing copy.

Recommendation: Meridian is the institution. The design system takes a subordinate name. This is also the cheapest route out of challenge 1, if you want one.

3 · Gate 1 was validated for the creator, not the recipient gate 1 stands

The transition reads as ceremony because you have learned the instrument register. You have watched that dark screen for years. The paper arriving means something because you know what it replaced.

The recipient has never seen the instrument register. They arrive cold, from a link, on a phone, with no account. For them there is no register change, because there was no register — they see a dark screen for a few seconds and then a letter.

That may still be good; the physical grammar of a seal opening needs no product literacy. But it is a different experience than the one we validated, and it belongs to the person the moment is for.

Action: add Gate 5 — the cold recipient path. Tested with someone who has never seen the product, on a real cheap Android, from a real link. It outranks gates 2–4.

4 · Sound: "default off" becomes "never" changed

Part VII. The implementation note in 06 is superseded.

5 · Elapsed-and-remaining breaks at both ends refined

On day one of a ten-year wait, "Travelled: 0 days" against "Remaining: 3,652 days" is demoralising — the exact impatience the principle exists to prevent. In the final hours, elapsed is emotionally irrelevant.

The Dual Clock's weighting shifts across the life of the capsule — remaining-led at the start, genuinely equal through the long middle, remaining-led again at the approach. Both figures always present; the emphasis moves. The component becomes an instrument that changes with time, which is Principle 2 applied to the thing carrying the most brand.

6 · Proof of life: build it decided

Left open in 03. It is the only answer to decade-long silence, and silence is the failure mode of the entire trust asymmetry.

7 · Priority order changes changed

05 scheduled logo → design system → T-21 → web → mobile. A perfect logo on a product nobody finishes writing in is worthless.

Cold-recipient gate → composition experience → logo → design system → T-21 → web → mobile.

Part XVII · North Star
The filter that outranks the others

Nobody says "what a beautiful app."
They say "I can't believe it actually came."

The product succeeds by being forgotten in favour of its contents. The highest possible outcome is that someone reads a letter from a person they have lost, is undone by it, and cannot afterwards describe a single thing about the interface.

This is an uncomfortable north star for a design team, which is exactly why it is the right one. Our craft is measured by how little of it is noticed — and it makes every decision testable:

Does this make the letter matter more, or does it make us matter more? If the second, it does not ship, regardless of how good it is.

The three filters, in order: 1. Does this make the promise more believable? 2. Does the waiting state look like something valuable is happening? 3. Does this make the letter matter more than us?
