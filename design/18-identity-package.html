Meridian V2 · Phase 10 · Identity package

The Transit, refined

Four rejected refinements, one that worked, and the production assets. The mark now carries a notch — a place reserved in the meridian, exactly the size of the marker coming to fill it.

The diagnosis

Why the old mark failed

○ | was two isolated objects with no relationship beyond adjacency. There was no single figure to remember, so at small sizes it collapsed into whatever nearby glyph it most resembled: a bullet and a rule, a slider handle, a record button.

The fix could not be addition — a third element breaks 16px. It had to make the two existing elements belong to each other.

The notch does that. The meridian is broken exactly where the marker will arrive, and the break is exactly the marker's diameter. The two elements are now cut for one another — a key and its keyway. Neither reads as complete without the other, which is precisely the emotional idea: a moment already set aside, and something still on its way to it.

Iterations

Six refinements, five rejected

#RefinementResult
R2Lozenge marker instead of a circleRejected — at 16px the diamond becomes a dot and the mark is the baseline again. No gain where it was needed.
R3Notch, segments ≈ notch heightRejected — 1:1 ratio reads as a colon at 16px. Right idea, wrong proportion.
R4Notch + open ring markerRejected — the ring's counter closes at 16px and the marker becomes a blob.
R5Marker touching the notchRejected — reads as arrival, not approach. The brand lives in the years before.
R9Half-depth notch (a step, not a break)Rejected — the notch vanishes by 16px. The feature carrying the meaning must not be the first casualty of scaling.
R10Tapered meridian, heavier at the notchRejected — invisible at every size that matters. Complexity for nothing.
R7Notch at a 2.1 : 1 segment ratioAdopted. The line dominates, the notch reads as a bite taken out of it, and both survive to 16px.

The ratio is the whole refinement. At 1:1 the mark is a colon; at 3:1 the notch is a nick. 2.1:1 is the only window where a viewer reads “one line, interrupted” rather than “two marks” or “one mark”. It is the number to protect.

Registers

One mark, no variants

Blind emboss

No ink — only a light edge and a shadow edge. Two forms and a uniform stroke is the easiest possible case for a die, and the notch gives the embosser a feature to register against.

Lockups

Mark and wordmark

TimelyCapsule
TimelyCapsule

The gap is 1.2× the mark's width — wider than optical default. At a normal gap the notched meridian reads as punctuation attached to the word. This was the one weakness applied-identity testing found, and spacing fixes it.

The <text> in the lockup SVGs must be outlined before production use. A live text element falls back to a system face in most email clients, many PDF viewers, and every partner deck.

Open

What I have not settled

  • The wordmark still commits you to the name. The mark is rename-proof and can ship today; the lockups should be treated as provisional until 09 §XIV.1 is resolved.
  • Emboss stroke weight is untested on a real press. The guideline says +15%; that is an instinct, not a measurement. Proof before any print run.
  • RTL reading is untested. A left-approaching marker may read as departing in Arabic or Hebrew contexts. Not blocking — 05 limits us to Latin-plus.
  • Nobody outside this project has seen it. The cheapest useful test remains showing it to five people cold and asking only “what is this?”
