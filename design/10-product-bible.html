Meridian · Strategic Foundation · Phase 09

The Product Bible

Behaviour, not features. What role this plays in a human life over thirty years — and the historical evidence that we are currently building toward the wrong failure.

Part I · The finding

Our competitor is oblivion, and it wins nine times in ten

International Time Capsule Society
80–90% of physical time capsules are never recovered. An estimated 95% are lost track of within five years of being sealed.

The containers do not fail. The concrete holds, the documents survive. What fails is that nobody remembers the capsule exists — the people move away, the building is replaced, the institution forgets.

We have been engineering trust against the wrong threat. Institutional survival — backups, escrow, restore drills, "will the company exist" — is a real risk and that work was correct. But it is not the likeliest failure.

The likeliest failure: in 2041 the capsule is intact, the servers are running, the encryption is sound, the delivery job fires exactly on schedule — and the email address died in 2033.

We deliver perfectly, into a void, and never know. A recipient address captured once in 2026 and never touched again is a fifteen-year bet on an email account. Nobody should take that bet.

The consequence: proof of life becomes load-bearing

07 specified proof of life as a trust gesture — still here, still counting, nothing for you to do. That was right for the wrong reason. Its real job is address validation, and it detects a dead route ten years before it matters, while there is still a living person to ask for a new one.

The gesture and the infrastructure turn out to be the same object. That upgrades proof of life from nice to mandatory.

Part II · What we are

A correspondent, not a destination

Not an app someone uses. A standing arrangement someone has made, touched perhaps twenty times in three decades. The true analogues are not software.

Every durable thirty-year arrangement in a human life is kept alive by a third party who periodically makes contact — never by the person remembering.

Part III · Tradition audit

We score zero out of five

Journals, photo albums, annual letters, ornaments, baby books, memory boxes. Five properties recur, and family-ritual research supports all five. Scored honestly against what we have designed so far:

Every recommendation below is an attempt to move one of these rows.

Part IV · Frequency

The thing we cannot fake

A normal person encounters a natural reason to seal a capsule one to three times per decade. Habit products require frequency. We have none — and every standard remedy for low frequency is a pattern we permanently banned in 07.

So we stop pursuing retention, which is impossible, and pursue recurrence: being present, and remembered, at the moments that occur anyway.

Eight of the nine are external and outside our control. The ninth is the only one we can build.

The annual rite — the highest-leverage item in this document

One deliberate occasion a year, on a date the household chooses, where the family writes to itself twelve months out. Last year's arrives as this year's is written.

It is the only mechanism that fixes four of the five audit rows at once — calendar anchor, accumulation, low cost, collective sign-on. It is culturally precedented (the annual Christmas letter, New Year resolutions, the school "letter to your future self"). And it requires no nagging, because the arrival is the reminder. The product does not prompt; last year's capsule does.

This should be built before the marketing site.

The Future Self is the acquisition archetype; the Parent is the retention archetype. A product tuned only for the Parent has no on-ramp. One tuned only for the Future Self never becomes a tradition. We need both, and they want opposite tones.

The fact we can never know

We do not know whether the sender is alive when the capsule arrives.

We cannot know, we must not ask, and the two cases are emotionally opposite. "Your father has a message for you" delivered to someone whose father died last year is a catastrophic failure — and we would never find out we had done it.

Absolute rule, joining the copy constitution: all delivery language is past tense and refers only to the act of sealing, never to the sender's present state.

AlwaysNever
"Sealed by Michael Adeyemi on 14 March 2026.""Michael has sent you a capsule."
"This was written for you in 2026.""A message is waiting from Michael."

It costs nothing and prevents the worst thing the product could do.

Part VII · Sacred and ordinary

Sanctity is spent only at the boundary between private and permanent

Sacred — five things onlyOrdinary — everything else

If everything is sacred, nothing is. A solemn interface around a note to yourself in six months is absurd — and worse, it makes the trivial capsule too expensive to write, removing the on-ramp that produces the Parent five years later.

The rare, devastating capsule lands because it sits among ordinary ones. Gravity requires contrast.

Part VIII · Growth

The guarantee we could not break if we wanted to

Zero-access encryption means we cannot read capsule contents — therefore we can never build a feed.

Not a policy we might reverse under investor pressure. Arithmetic. It is the most credible commitment in the product, because it is the only one we are incapable of breaking.

  1. 01Arrival → new creator. Every delivered capsule produces a candidate creator who has just experienced the product at full strength. Slow, measured in years, compounding.
  2. 02The witnessed arrival. Someone opens a letter at a table with others present. Invisible to us; probably our largest channel.
  3. 03The duration-only share artifact. Content-blind by construction.
  4. 04Institutions. A school seals 200 capsules in an afternoon.

This reframes P8. The recipient path was justified on decency grounds. It is also the acquisition funnel, and deserves the most engineering rather than the least — the opposite of how a normal product resources an unauthenticated read-only page.

Never: profiles, followers, feeds, likes, public galleries, capsule counts, leaderboards, or any surface where users encounter strangers.

Part IX · Institutions

They solve our hardest problem for free

A school holds alumni records and has a permanent address. The institution is the custodian, and institutional memory outlives every individual in it. A school cohort capsule is more likely to be delivered than a private one.

1 · Death-triggered release is the most requested and most dangerous feature we will ever be asked for. A false positive delivers a dead person's final letters to their living family. Automated death detection is not acceptable at any confidence level. If built at all it must be custodian-confirmed — a named human asserting the death — never inferred from inactivity, a data broker, or a missed check-in.

2 · Public capsules break our cryptography. A capsule for future historians is not addressed to a person and is not zero-access — anyone may open it. Different threat model, different key architecture, different product. Do not quietly blend it into the private one.

Parts X–XI · Barriers

Psychological

  1. 01The blank page. Already the largest gap in 07. Top of this list too.
  2. 02Superstition about the future. Writing to your child at eighteen means contemplating not being there. Many will start and close the tab. No interface fixes this — only tone. Never make mortality explicit; let the person bring it.
  3. 03"Who am I to think this matters?" People badly underestimate what a letter from a dead parent will mean. The only persuasive answer is testimony from someone who received one — which we will not have for years, and must not fake.
  4. 04Fear of being embarrassed by your past self. Mitigated by making short, low-stakes capsules normal.
  5. 05Choice paralysis on the date. Offer anchored answers — when she turns eighteen — not a date picker alone.

Trust

  1. 01"Will you exist?" — already the centre of the marketing plan.
  2. 02"Will you be able to find them?" — unaddressed, and likelier to bite than the first.
  3. 03"What if I lose the key?" Our most self-inflicted risk. The person who loses it will reasonably experience this as us having destroyed their letters.
  4. 04"Can you read it?" Answerable with architecture. Our strongest answer.
  5. 05"What happens when I die?" Nobody can inherit an account today. For a product about outliving people, a conspicuous hole.
  6. 06"What if I change my mind?" — Part XIV.4.
Part XII · Behavioural risks

How we will go wrong

  • We will over-report our own health. Four of six churn reasons are invisible.
  • We will optimise for the creator, because the creator is who we can see. The recipient generates almost no telemetry and is the more important person.
  • We will manufacture frequency the moment growth flattens. Every remedy that is not the annual rite is a banned pattern wearing a hat.
  • The archive will silently become a to-do list. A list of pending items with dates is a task manager — the opposite of the intended feeling. It must read as a shelf, not a queue.
  • The product will drift solemn. Every decision in 01–08 pushes toward gravity; nothing pushes back. The Future Self is our only counterweight and is currently unserved.
Part XIII · Thirty years

Success

  • Someone opens a letter from a parent who died fifteen years earlier, and it is the most valuable object they own.
  • A family has a date. Every year, the same evening, everyone writes. Children who grew up with it start doing it in their own households.
  • An account has been inherited — a custodian named, a transfer executed, the arrangement continuing past the death of the person who made it.
  • A school opens the capsules its 2027 intake sealed, and it is on the calendar as an institution, not a project someone champions.
  • We are boring. Nobody discusses our design. The letters are discussed.

Failure — and it is not bankruptcy

The silent failure

We deliver flawlessly for thirty years into addresses that died in the 2030s. Every job succeeds. Every dashboard is green. Nobody receives anything.

We become the 90%, with better uptime.

The second failure is likelier to be self-inflicted. We solve low frequency the way software always solves low frequency — a nudge here, a streak there, an anniversary email, a feed of public capsules. Each defensible alone. Collectively they convert a thirty-year arrangement into an app people churn out of in eight weeks.

Part XIV · Challenges to 01–08

Seven

1 · Trust engineering aims at the wrong threat new · biggest

Institutional survival is centred everywhere; deliverability appears nowhere. Proof of life is promoted from gesture to infrastructure. Multiple contact routes and periodic re-verification become requirements.

2 · Uniform ceremony is wrong refines 07

I wrote that the held beat applies to everything irreversible. Applied uniformly it makes a six-month note as solemn as a deathbed letter — which cheapens the serious capsule and taxes the trivial one out of existence.

Refinement, not reversal: consequence is not binary. Ceremony scales with duration × relationship. The principle — duration is a function of consequence — is unchanged; I had been treating a continuous variable as a switch.

3 · The product is too solemn, and has no on-ramp challenges 01–08

Every foundational document pushes toward gravity. Right for the Parent and the Terminal; wrong for the Future Self, where nearly everyone starts. Nothing in the identity currently makes room for a light capsule. Resolve before component design — it is a tone and hierarchy question, not a screen question.

4 · Immutability and irrevocability are being conflated founder decision

Sealed content must remain immutable. Nothing here proposes changing that. But immutability of content is a different property from irrevocability of delivery, and we have treated them as one.

Someone seals a letter to their spouse for their twenty-fifth anniversary. They divorce in year eight. The letter will be delivered. We become a machine that reliably delivers letters from marriages that ended.

Proposed resolution — a right of withdrawal: a creator may destroy a sealed capsule before delivery; they may never alter it. Stricter than most archival practice, and it preserves the cryptographic guarantee exactly: records may be destroyed; they may never be forged. It does not weaken the promise, which is made to the creator — the recipient does not know the capsule exists.

5 · P8 is the funnel, not a courtesy reframes 01 / 07

The recipient path should be resourced as the most important surface in the product, not the most constrained.

6 · There is no inheritance model gap

For a product about outliving people, nobody can inherit an account. Needs a named custodian at minimum — also the honest answer to trust barrier 5, and a precondition for the memorial use cases.

7 · The annual rite precedes the marketing site changes 07

It is the only calendar anchor available to us and the only honest answer to our frequency problem — and it changes what the marketing site is selling.

Revised order: cold-recipient gate → composition experience → annual rite → logo → design system → T-21 → web → mobile.

Part XV · The one sentence
Everything above, reduced

We are not a place people go. We are a correspondent who keeps writing to them, so that in thirty years the letter is not lost.

The central job is not storage, encryption, or ceremony — those are solved. The central job is to still be able to find the person.

The four filters, final:

  1. 01Does this make the promise more believable? 02
  2. 02Does the waiting state look like something valuable is happening? 03
  3. 03Does this make the letter matter more than us? 07
  4. 04Does this make it more likely we can still find them in 2041? 09
Part XVI · What implementation inherits

Eight product requirements

  1. 01Multiple contact routes per recipient, captured at creation.
  2. 02Periodic address re-verification, doubling as proof of life.
  3. 03Escalation to the creator when a route goes dark.
  4. 04A named custodian per account — inheritance, and the only acceptable basis for any post-death release.
  5. 05The annual rite.
  6. 06A right of withdrawal — destroy, never alter. Pending founder decision.
  7. 07Short-form capsules as a first-class, deliberately un-ceremonious path.
  8. 08An archive that reads as a shelf, not a queue.

Design work this creates: ceremony that scales with duration × relationship · a tonal register for the ordinary capsule · past-tense-only delivery language, absolutely · accumulation made visible.
